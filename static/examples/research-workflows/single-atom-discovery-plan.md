# Preliminary reading plan: single-atom catalysis

## Journal-club question

**What evidence connects isolated metal sites to useful catalytic performance?**

This is a preliminary 60-minute reading plan based only on the bibliographic metadata and abstracts retrieved during the topic search. It does not rely on full-text findings, supplementary information, page numbers, or figure references.

## Recommended five-paper set

### 1. DeRita et al. (2017): separating single atoms from clusters

**Citation:** Leo DeRita; Sheng Dai; Kimberly Lopez-Zepeda; Nicholas Pham; George W. Graham; Xiaoqing Pan; Phillip Christopher. “Catalyst Architecture for Stable Single Atom Dispersion Enables Site-Specific Spectroscopic and Reactivity Measurements of CO Adsorbed to Pt Atoms, Oxidized Pt Clusters, and Metallic Pt Clusters on TiO₂.” *Journal of the American Chemical Society* (2017). [https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.7b07093](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.7b07093)

**Reason for inclusion:** The retrieved abstract frames a central attribution problem in single-atom catalysis: heterogeneous samples may contain isolated atoms, oxidized clusters, and metallic particles. The reported catalyst architecture and site-specific spectroscopy are therefore a strong starting point for asking what evidence is needed before performance can be assigned to isolated Pt atoms.

**Reading question:** What comparison or measurement would most convincingly rule out clusters or nanoparticles as the source of the observed reactivity?

### 2. Lang et al. (2019): thermal stability linked to useful oxidation

**Citation:** Rui Lang; Wei Xi; Jin-Cheng Liu; Yi-Tao Cui; Tianbo Li; Adam Fraser Lee; Fang Chen; Yang Chen; Lei Li; Lin Li; Jian Lin; Shu Miao; Xiaoyan Liu; Ai-Qin Wang; Xiaodong Wang; Jun Luo; Botao Qiao; Jun Li; Tao Zhang. “Non defect-stabilized thermally stable single-atom catalyst.” *Nature Communications* (2019). [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-08136-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-08136-3)

**Reason for inclusion:** The abstract connects a proposed covalent metal–support interaction, isolated Pt on Fe₂O₃, high metal loading, thermal stability, and methane-combustion performance. It lets the group examine whether evidence for remaining atomically dispersed under harsh conditions is sufficiently connected to a practically relevant catalytic reaction.

**Reading question:** Which evidence is needed to show that isolated Pt persists during methane combustion, rather than only before or after the reaction?

### 3. Li et al. (2020): direct performance benchmark against nanoparticles

**Citation:** Zhi Li; Yuanjun Chen; Shufang Ji; Yan Tang; Wenxing Chen; Ang Li; Jie Zhao; Yu Xiong; Yuen Wu; Yue Gong; Tao Yao; Wei Liu; Lirong Zheng; Juncai Dong; Yu Wang; Zhongbin Zhuang; Wei Xing; Chun-Ting He; Chao Peng; Weng-Chon Cheong; Qiheng Li; Maolin Zhang; Zheng Chen; Ninghua Fu; Xin Gao; Wei Zhu; Jiawei Wan; Jian Zhang; Lin Gu; Shiqiang Wei; Peijun Hu; Jun Luo; Jun Li; Chen Chen; Qing Peng; Xiangfeng Duan; Yu Huang; Xiao-Ming Chen; Dingsheng Wang; Yadong Li. “Iridium single-atom catalyst on nitrogen-doped carbon for formic acid oxidation synthesized using a general host–guest strategy.” *Nature Chemistry* (2020). [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-020-0473-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-020-0473-9)

**Reason for inclusion:** The retrieved abstract reports a general host–guest synthesis and compares Ir₁/N–C with Ir nanoparticles and commercial Pd/C and Pt/C catalysts. It attributes high formic-acid-oxidation mass activity and CO tolerance to spatial isolation and altered Ir electronic structure, giving the club a concrete performance comparison to interrogate.

**Reading question:** Does the nanoparticle benchmark isolate the effect of atomic dispersion, or could differences in support, loading, coordination, or electronic structure still explain the performance gap?

### 4. Lu et al. (2021): operando evidence for a catalytic cycle

**Citation:** Yubing Lu; Shulan Zhou; Chun-Te Kuo; Deepak Kunwar; Coogan Thompson; Adam S. Hoffman; Alexey Boubnov; Sen Lin; Abhaya K. Datye; Hua Guo; Ayman M. Karim. “Unraveling the Intermediate Reaction Complexes and Critical Role of Support-Derived Oxygen Atoms in CO Oxidation on Single-Atom Pt/CeO₂.” *ACS Catalysis* (2021). [https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.1c01900](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.1c01900)

**Reason for inclusion:** The abstract describes a catalyst beginning with exclusively isolated Pt and combines in situ/operando spectroscopy, calorimetry, kinetics, and DFT. It reports two Pt₁ complexes during CO oxidation and a role for support-derived oxygen, making it the strongest mechanism-focused candidate in the retrieved set.

**Reading question:** Which parts of the proposed Pt₁ reaction cycle are directly observed, and which remain interpretations that depend on kinetics or computation?

### 5. Hai et al. (2022 issue; online 2021): scalable, automated high-density libraries

**Citation:** Xiao Hai; Shibo Xi; Sharon Mitchell; Karim Harrath; Haomin Xu; Dario Faust Akl; Debin Kong; Jing Li; Zejun Li; Tao Sun; Huimin Yang; Yige Cui; Chenliang Su; Xiaoxu Zhao; Jun Li; Javier Pérez-Ramírez; Jiong Lu. “Scalable two-step annealing method for preparing ultra-high-density single-atom catalyst libraries.” *Nature Nanotechnology* (2022 issue; published online 2021). [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-021-01022-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-021-01022-y)

**Reason for inclusion:** The abstract reports a standardized two-step annealing method, high single-atom densities, mono- and multimetallic libraries, and translation to an automated protocol. Experiments and simulations are said to connect stepwise ligand removal with suppressed aggregation, while catalytic testing addresses whether scalable materials remain functional.

**Reading question:** What additional evidence would be required to move from a scalable automated protocol to a credible claim of industrially relevant scale-up?

## Suggested 60-minute structure

- **0–5 minutes:** Define the claim under review: isolated sites must be identified, shown to persist under relevant conditions, and connected causally to performance.
- **5–15 minutes:** DeRita et al.—site assignment and competing active species.
- **15–25 minutes:** Lang et al.—thermal stability under a useful oxidation reaction.
- **25–35 minutes:** Li et al.—single atoms versus nanoparticle and commercial benchmarks.
- **35–47 minutes:** Lu et al.—operando mechanism and the support’s role.
- **47–57 minutes:** Hai et al.—high density, automation, and scale-up claims.
- **57–60 minutes:** Rank the five papers by evidentiary strength and identify the most important missing experiment.

## Three reserve candidates

The remaining verified candidates are useful reserves:

- **Yao et al. (2019),** “High temperature shockwave stabilized single atoms,” [10.1038/s41565-019-0518-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-019-0518-7): alternative rapid-synthesis and high-temperature-stability case.
- **Liu et al. (2020),** “Strong metal-support interaction promoted scalable production of thermally stable single-atom catalysts,” [10.1038/s41467-020-14984-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-14984-9): alternative scale-up and metal–support-interaction case.
- **Yan et al. (2020),** “Temperature-Induced Structure Reconstruction to Prepare a Thermally Stable Single-Atom Platinum Catalyst,” [10.1002/anie.202004929](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202004929): alternative severe thermal-stability case.

## Evidence limitations

Only metadata and abstracts were retrieved. The plan therefore does not claim that any paper’s full methods, figures, controls, supplementary data, durability tests, or scale metrics establish more than the abstract states. In particular, the retrieved evidence does not establish kilogram-scale manufacture, commercial deployment, or long-duration industrial operation.
