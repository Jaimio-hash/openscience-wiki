# Single-Atom Catalysis: Journal-Club Reading Pack

The five papers connect site synthesis and stabilization to mechanistic assignment and reactor translation. Performance values below are interpreted only within each paper’s own reaction and conditions; efficiencies are not ranked across different reactions.

**Evidence basis.** This pack was prepared from the supplied page-labelled text extracted from the same five original publisher PDFs. PDF page numbers below are the page indices in that supplied text. The original PDFs remain available for visual checking of figures. Where text extraction did not expose a figure label clearly, the PDF-page locator is used instead; supplementary evidence not present in the supplied main-paper text is identified as such.

## Five papers

### Lang et al. (2019)

**Title:** Non defect-stabilized thermally stable single-atom catalyst  
**Authors/source:** Rui Lang, Wei Xi, Jin-Cheng Liu et al.; *Nature Communications* 10, 234 (2019)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-018-08136-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-08136-3)

**Research question.** Can high-loading Pt single atoms remain thermally stable without relying on pre-existing support defects?

**Catalyst/reaction.** Pt single atoms on Fe₂O₃, with extension to Fe₂O₃-doped non-reducible supports; CO oxidation is used as a catalytic test.

**Supported finding.** Increasing Pt loading to 2 wt% produced clusters alongside isolated atoms, consistent with a stated theoretical maximum of about 1.5 wt% for Fe₂O₃ with a surface area of 10 m² g⁻¹. The authors therefore link the maximum atomically dispersed loading to available support surface area (**PDF page 4; Supplementary Fig. 8c–d is cited in the text but is not contained in the supplied main-paper extraction**).

**Limitation.** The attainable isolated-atom loading is support-area and anchoring-site dependent, and broad transfer beyond the tested Pt/iron-oxide chemistry is not established by this paper alone.

**Discussion questions.**

1. Which evidence best distinguishes defect trapping from reducibility-enabled covalent metal–support interaction?
2. How would an operando experiment test whether the calcined isolated-Pt population remains the catalytic population?

### Yang et al. (2019)

**Title:** A universal ligand mediated method for large scale synthesis of transition metal single atom catalysts  
**Authors/source:** Hongzhou Yang, Lu Shang, Qinghua Zhang et al.; *Nature Communications* 10, 4585 (2019)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-019-12510-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12510-0)

**Research question.** Can one practical ligand-mediated method produce a broad library of carbon-supported transition-metal SACs at high loading and kilogram scale?

**Catalyst/reaction.** Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn, Ru, Pt, and multimetal SACs on commercial carbon black, made using 1,10-phenanthroline and 600 °C pyrolysis; Ni-SAC is tested for electrochemical CO₂ reduction to CO.

**Supported finding.** Ni-SAC maintained CO Faradaic efficiency above 90% from −0.7 to −1.5 V versus RHE and reached 98.9% at −1.2 V (**Figure 6b, PDF page 6**). The same page reports only a slight current-density decrease during a 20 h test at −0.8 V (**Figure 6c, PDF page 6**).

**Limitation.** “Universal” describes the demonstrated synthesis family. Catalytic validation is concentrated on Ni-SAC CO₂ reduction, so equivalent reactivity and durability are not shown for every synthesized metal.

**Discussion questions.**

1. Which controls separate the role of 1,10-phenanthroline in atomic isolation from its role as a nitrogen source?
2. At kilogram scale, which variable—adsorption, mixing, heat transfer, or gas flow—is most likely to broaden the site distribution?

### Liu et al. (2017)

**Title:** High performance platinum single atom electrocatalyst for oxygen reduction reaction  
**Authors/source:** Jing Liu, Menggai Jiao, Lanlu Lu et al.; *Nature Communications* 8, 15938 (2017)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/ncomms15938](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15938)

**Correction check.** The publisher lists an erratum for this paper. Consult that erratum alongside the article before relying on corrected details; its content is not guessed or summarized here.

**Research question.** Can isolated Pt on nitrogen-doped carbon reduce Pt use in an acidic ORR fuel-cell cathode while retaining activity, durability, and CO/methanol tolerance?

**Catalyst/reaction.** Pt₁–N/BP carbon-black catalyst; acidic and alkaline oxygen reduction plus an acidic single-cell cathode test.

**Supported finding.** The paper reports an acidic single-cell power density up to 680 mW cm⁻² at 80 °C with a cathode Pt loading of 0.09 mgPt cm⁻² (**PDF page 1; the single-cell panel is Figure 2f on PDF page 4**). Separately, Pt₁–N/BP shifted only 3 mV in half-wave potential after 10,000 accelerated CV cycles (**Figure 2c; result discussed on PDF page 5**).

**Limitation.** The proposed pyridinic-N-anchored Pt active site is inferred from ensemble characterization and model calculations; the current-carrying local motif is not directly counted under fuel-cell operation.

**Discussion questions.**

1. Which operando measurement could most directly test the proposed pyridinic-N–Pt₁ motif?
2. Does CO/methanol tolerance uniquely support that motif, or could support chemistry and transport contribute?

### Hossain et al. (2020)

**Title:** Reaction mechanism and kinetics for CO₂ reduction on nickel single atom catalysts from quantum mechanics  
**Authors/source:** Md Delowar Hossain, Yufeng Huang, Ted H. Yu, William A. Goddard III, and Zhengtang Luo; *Nature Communications* 11, 2256 (2020)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-020-16119-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-16119-6)

**Research question.** How do Ni–N₂C₂, Ni–N₃C₁, and Ni–N₄ coordination motifs control potential-dependent CO₂-to-CO kinetics and competing hydrogen evolution?

**Catalyst/reaction.** Graphene-embedded Ni single-site models; electrochemical CO₂ reduction to CO and hydrogen evolution, modeled with grand-canonical potential kinetics.

**Supported finding.** The calculated CO₂RR onset at 10 mA cm⁻² is −0.84 V for Ni–N₂C₂; at that potential the predicted CO Faradaic efficiency is 98% and TOF is 3903 h⁻¹. The model then predicts different motifs dominate at more negative potentials (**Figure 7b–d, PDF page 8**).

**Limitation.** These are predictions for idealized sites with modeled solvation and kinetics. Site heterogeneity, electrode transport, and the actual operando coordination distribution require experimental validation.

**Discussion questions.**

1. Which predicted observable—XPS shift, CO vibration, Tafel slope, or onset potential—best distinguishes the three motifs experimentally?
2. How might local pH, solvent structure, field strength, and mixed sites alter the predicted potential windows?

### Chen et al. (2022)

**Title:** Addressing the quantitative conversion bottleneck in single-atom catalysis  
**Authors/source:** Zhongxin Chen, Jingting Song, Rongrong Zhang et al.; *Nature Communications* 13, 2807 (2022)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-022-30551-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-30551-w)

**Research question.** Can a mechanically robust SAC-coated flow module overcome productivity and leaching constraints in liquid-phase SAC transformations?

**Catalyst/reaction.** Pt₁–MoS₂ on graphite felt in a fuel-cell-type flow stack for chemoselective nitroarene hydrogenation; Co₁–MoS₂ is also tested for sulfide oxidation.

**Supported finding.** At 0.2 M reactant concentration, the benchtop module reached a reported maximum aniline productivity of 5.8 g h⁻¹, corresponding to a TOF of about 8000 h⁻¹ (**PDF page 4; Supplementary Fig. 21 is cited but not contained in the supplied main-paper extraction**). A separate low-conversion stability test showed no significant decay over 12 h (**Figure 4e; text on PDF page 4 and caption on PDF page 5**).

**Limitation.** This is a benchtop, hours-scale demonstration. Manufacturing-scale pressure drop, heat and mass transfer, catalyst replacement, hydrogen/solvent handling, and process economics remain untested.

**Discussion questions.**

1. Which result most cleanly separates intrinsic single-site chemistry from reactor-enhanced mass transfer?
2. Which scale-up quantity should be conserved first: residence-time distribution, site throughput, pressure drop, heat removal, or selectivity?

## Sixty-minute discussion order

**0–5 min — Common frame.** Define the evidence needed to call a site “single atom” and agree not to compare efficiency numbers across unlike reactions.

**5–17 min — Synthesis breadth: Yang et al.** Examine ligand-mediated preparation, kilogram-scale claims, site uniformity, and the corrected Figure 6 CO₂RR locator.

**17–29 min — Stability and loading: Lang et al.** Contrast ligand control with oxidative redistribution and metal–support interaction; focus on the loading ceiling and cluster formation.

**29–40 min — Site assignment in a device: Liu et al.** Flag the publisher erratum check first, then connect ORR characterization to fuel-cell behavior and question the proposed Pt–N motif.

**40–50 min — Mechanism: Hossain et al.** Ask how coordination changes CO₂RR/HER kinetics and which predictions can discriminate sites experimentally.

**50–60 min — Scale-up: Chen et al.** Separate kinetic and transport contributions, assess the hours-scale stability evidence, and define the next process-scale experiment.

**Closing prompt:** If synthesis establishes a site distribution, operation changes its stability, and reactor transport changes the apparent kinetics, what minimum operando and process evidence is needed before calling a SAC process scalable?
